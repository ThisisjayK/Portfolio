Case study · Stage Zero Health

Technical PM co-op · Aug–Dec 2025 · Cambridge, MA · B2C

01

Earning trust for a cancer risk score.

Stage Zero Health is an early-stage startup helping people understand their breast cancer risk and what to do next. I joined as a technical product manager co-op and worked across the whole breast cancer journey, from discovery to a paid pilot.

This is the story of one problem I found, owned and shipped: people didn't trust digital health.

02

What I heard

US healthcare was new to me, so every customer interview taught me something: how insurance and claims work, how much of care falls on family caregivers.

People were deeply aware of their health. Most were skeptical of digital health.

Skepticism, not awareness, was the real barrier.

03

My bet: calm and checkable

A risk score that feels alarming, or that you can't verify, gets ignored. I proposed the opposite, and owned it end to end:

  • Raised the skepticism from the interviews with the team
  • Wrote the requirements for how scores and recommendations were shown: calm and non-alarming
  • Linked to original sources or verified facts wherever people needed to check
  • Designed the wireframes, then tested them with users

What users taught me

06

Into a paid pilot

We shipped the risk score, explanation and referral journey to our waitlist and beta cohorts, then ran a 6-week paid pilot.

  • Weekly surveys, Zoom calls and demos with pilot users, feeding the backlog
  • Pilot users signed off before release; I wrote the test plans and user docs
  • Supported the founder's go-to-market: launch, positioning, pricing, target users

Also owned

AI assistant prompts and evals · health and genetic report extraction · risk-model spec and API contracts · FHIR pre-fill and insurance verification

And

TAM/SAM/SOM and competitive research · KPI dashboard · building under HIPAA

07

What I took away

  • Trust is a feature. You design it, test it and ship it like anything else.
  • I started taking ownership more seriously, and collaborating more widely across the team.
  • I learned what it takes to build a company from the ground up, and how a startup decides what matters.
  • Every day was different and a little messy. I loved it.
